Research

Sim-to-real transfer for high-gear-ratio quadrupeds

Ph.D. thesis · Machines 2026 · ICRA 2025 (Late Breaking Results)

Quasi-direct-drive legs are transparent and easy to simulate; high-reduction legs are not. Above a 40:1 ratio, gearbox friction, motor nonlinearity and control delay dominate the joint response, and a policy trained on an ideal actuator falls over on the real robot. I attack that gap from both ends:

  • a BLDC motor model in simulation, plus a time-delayed network that learns actuator dynamics implicitly from short-term joint history;
  • an extended friction model whose parameters are fitted by CMA-ES in a real-sim-real loop, cutting the simulation-to-hardware joint trajectory error by up to 42% against prior work;
  • a GRU height estimator that infers local terrain from proprioception alone, taking the same policy over 20 cm steps and continuous stairs.

ARS: recovery and self-righting from a single network

First author · Biomimetics, 2024

Getting a fallen quadruped back on its feet is contact-rich enough that predicting the contact sequence in advance is impractical, so scripted trajectories break down. ARS trains per-behavior policies with a behavior selector, then distills all of them into one network with a DAGGER-like scheme. The single network is cheaper to run online than the multi-network baseline, recovers more smoothly, and generalizes to terrain it never saw in training — 82% success upright, 58% from fully upside-down.

AiDIN quadruped platforms

Hardware and whole-body control · IEEE T-IE 2020, ICRA 2019, IROS 2017-2019

Before the learning work I built robots. I contributed to the AiDIN series at SKKU — torque-controllable modular actuator units, capacitive joint torque sensors, the ROS-based sensing stack (IMU, encoders, stereo camera), and whole-body control for stair climbing and balance recovery — including a joint SKKU-UCLA effort.

Ongoing: perceptive and bipedal locomotion

Proprioception alone means the robot only learns about the terrain after it has touched it. I am training controllers that consume the raw depth image directly — no elevation map, and therefore no localization stack to keep it aligned — and extending the same actuator-identification approach to bipeds.
